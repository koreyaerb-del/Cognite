01 / Approach

How I'd build the list

Not a scraped list of large manufacturers. A list built on three filters: does the asset base match Cognite's product, has the company publicly committed to a number this problem affects, and can I name the executive who owns that number.

Continuous process, not discrete

Chemicals, refining, metals, pulp and paper, power. Multiple sites, brownfield equipment, a historian per plant, and a maintenance system that doesn't talk to it. That's where Cognite Data Fusion earns its keep — before auto or electronics assembly.

A public cost or reliability commitment

Process manufacturers are in a margin downturn. Capex is capped at maintenance levels, headcount is down, and boards have been promised EBITDA improvement that now has to come out of the existing asset base with fewer people. Funded, board-visible, time-boxed — the best kind of problem to prospect into.

I read earnings calls for the word "reliability"

When a CEO tells the market that two of three improvement pillars are ahead of plan and reliability is the one that isn't, that is no longer a cold call. It's a company describing the use case, on the record, with a number attached.

Arizona is an advantage I'd use

Cognite's US headquarters is in Tempe and I'm in Arizona. Freeport-McMoRan (Phoenix HQ), SRP (Tempe HQ), APS and Intel's Ocotillo campus are all in the same metro. A local BDR gets meetings a cold email doesn't — and an AE can ride along the same day.

A named owner, or it isn't Tier 1

Tier 1 means a verified trigger event, a verified executive who owns the outcome, and clean ICP fit. Tier 2 means strong fit with an unstable buying center or an unverified champion. Watchlist means large but currently blocked.

I'd rather write "unknown" than guess

Where I couldn't verify an executive against a company source, this page says so. Four titles carry a VERIFY tag because the last primary source is over a year old. A fabricated name in a CRM record is worse than a blank field.

02 / The market

What manufacturing clients are struggling with today

This list came first. Before I picked a single account I wrote down the twelve problems I hear underneath almost every manufacturing conversation — then went looking for companies that had said one of them out loud, in public, with a number attached. Each card below shows what a prospect actually says, what's really going on underneath it, and which accounts on my list it points to.

Why start with the pain instead of the company

Anyone can pull a list of large manufacturers. What makes a list worth an AE's time is knowing, before you dial, which specific problem you're calling about and why that company has it this quarter. Working pain-first is what turns a company name into a reason to call.

What twenty years of owning the company changes

I've sat in the seat these buyers sit in. I've had the month where capex got frozen, the key person who left with everything in their head, and the committee that had to agree before anything got signed. I don't have to guess what the objection will be — I've made it myself. That shortens discovery, and it means the AE gets the real blocker in the handoff instead of finding it on call two.

How this accelerates the sale

Most deals slow down because the first meeting spends forty minutes rediscovering what the problem is. If a BDR arrives having already mapped pain to a named owner to a funded commitment, the AE opens at the business case instead of at discovery. That's the whole value of doing this work up front — fewer meetings to the same decision.

06 / Standards

My bar, and how I'd run the week

A BDR's real currency is whether the AE clears their calendar when your name shows up on a handoff. Everything below protects that.

A name is not an opportunity

I don't hand over a contact who took a call. I hand over a confirmed pain, a named owner of that pain, a plausible funding path, and a next meeting already on the calendar.

Four things minimum before it moves

A specific operational problem in the prospect's own words. The executive who owns the outcome, by name. A reason it matters this quarter. A scheduled and accepted next meeting with the AE.

I'd rather be told my bar is too high

Than have an AE quietly stop trusting my handoffs. If the team wants volume over qualification, tell me and I'll adjust — but I'd want that to be a decision, not a drift.

Rejected handoffs are the best data I get

I'd ask every AE to mark each handoff accepted, recycled or rejected with one line of why, and I'd read that weekly. That loop is how a BDR gets better instead of just busier.

Inbound first, always

Speed to lead beats everything. Inbound gets worked before any outbound block opens — no exceptions.

Account plans co-owned with the AE

Stakeholder map, use-case hypothesis and a named reason to call now — reviewed together, not delivered as a surprise.

Events are a pipeline channel, not a booth shift

Pre-event outreach to a target list, meetings booked before arrival, follow-up out within 24 hours. I've presented at national tradeshows — this part I already know how to do.

CRM hygiene is the product, not admin

Every touch, every note, every next step logged the same day. The handoff above is only possible because the record underneath it is clean.

07 / Notes

Sourcing and method

On contact data. Every person named on this page is a real executive verified against a primary source — a company leadership page, press release, SEC filing or earnings-call transcript — as of September 2026. Where I could not verify someone, the page says no verified executive found.

I have deliberately not published direct emails or phone numbers here. Those get pulled at outreach time from ZoomInfo and Sales Navigator, validated against the company's email pattern, and written to Salesforce with a source and a date. They do not belong in a public document that goes stale in a month. Titles carrying a VERIFY tag have a primary source over a year old and I'd re-confirm before the first touch.

On the research itself. Trigger events come from dated primary sources: earnings-call transcripts, company press releases and SEC filings. Where a figure comes from a vendor's own marketing — including Cognite's — I've said so, because a number you can't defend in a discovery call is worse than no number.

What I don't know yet. Cognite's actual BDR process, qualification criteria, territory design, tooling configuration and handoff SLA. This page is what I'd propose on day one, not a claim about how the team works. I'd expect the real process to be better in ways I can't see from outside — and the fastest way for me to find out is to be told.
